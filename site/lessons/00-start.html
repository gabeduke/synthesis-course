Lesson 0

Start here

This course teaches synthesis on one small, cheap, surprisingly deep instrument: the M-VAVE FM-1, running Baud Girl’s FM-1+VA firmware. You don’t need any background. You do need ears, and ideally headphones.

What you need

  • A computer with Chrome or Edge. Every lesson makes sound and draws it right in the page. Firefox works for nearly everything; Safari plays the sounds but can’t talk to the FM-1.
  • Headphones or decent speakers. Laptop speakers hide most of the low end, and much of synthesis lives there.
  • An FM-1, if you have one. It’s optional. Every lesson works without it. With it, the page can play it, set its controls, and draw its real output next to the page’s version.
  • A USB-C cable that carries data, not just charging.

Getting the firmware

The FM-1 ships as an FM synthesizer. FM-1+VA is a free custom firmware by Baud Girl that keeps the FM engine and adds two more: Virtual Analog and 8-Bit. It also reworks the controls so you can reach everything with a few moves, and adds the knob banks this course relies on for live playing.

  1. Follow the install steps on Baud Girl’s install page. It runs in Chrome or Edge, takes under a minute, and keeps your saved presets.
  2. Open Baud Girl’s Device Manager and save a backup of your presets.
  3. Install the two free preset packs from the same page. The Virtual Analog pack goes into presets 113 to 128 and the 8-Bit pack into 97 to 112, replacing what was there, which is why the backup comes first.

This course was written against FM-1+VA version 97. The firmware updates often; when something here doesn’t match your FM-1, Baud Girl’s manual is the authority.

Four moves that reach every setting

Almost everything on the FM-1+VA happens with four controls. Learn these once and every lesson’s “On the FM-1” steps will make sense.

  • SELECT (knob) moves the highlight up and down a list.
  • ALGORITHM (knob) changes the value on the highlighted row.
  • SEL goes into whatever is highlighted.
  • HOME comes back out, one level at a time.

Then a handful of buttons jump straight to a screen: EDIT for the preset’s own settings, FX for its effects, ENV for its envelope, LFO for its LFO and GLO for settings of the whole FM-1. Turn PRESETS to choose a preset. KNOB1 to KNOB4 change four settings from any screen.

One habit to build early: a preset you’ve changed goes back to its stored version the moment you turn PRESETS, unless you press SAVE first. A dot after the preset’s name on the HOME screen means you have unsaved changes.

How the panels work

Each lesson has panels you play. They all work the same way:

  • Play the drawn keys with a mouse or finger, or use your computer keyboard: A to K are the white keys, the row above them the black keys, and Z and X shift the octave. The keyboard plays whichever panel you clicked last.
  • Hold a chord latches a chord so both hands are free for the sliders.
  • Hover over or tab to any slider and the line under the panel says where the same setting lives on the FM-1.
  • Color tells you what kind of thing a control is: blue shapes the sound itself, amber is about time, pink is movement, green is effects, and purple is always the FM-1’s real signal.

The page’s synth is a model of the FM-1’s Virtual Analog engine, built from the same parameter list and ranges. It’s close enough to learn from, but it won’t sound identical. That’s exactly why it’s useful to connect the real thing.

Connect your FM-1

The same panel is always in the top-right corner of every page, so you only need to do this once per visit. Your browser remembers the permission.

  • MIDI lets the page play notes on the FM-1 and change its settings. The browser will ask permission to use MIDI devices.
  • Audio lets the page listen to the FM-1, which is also a USB audio interface. Browsers call every audio input a microphone, so the permission prompt will say microphone. The page only analyzes the sound to draw it; nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

Once MIDI is connected, every panel gains three things:

  • Play on sends your notes to the page, the FM-1, or both.
  • Mirror to FM-1 makes each slider you move set the same control on the hardware, live.
  • Send settings to FM-1 copies the whole panel to the FM-1 in one go.

Those last two change the preset that’s selected on the FM-1, so pick a Virtual Analog preset first (113 to 128 from the pack). They never save: the firmware has no MIDI control for SAVE, and this page never sends the kind of patch data that would overwrite a preset, so nothing changes for good until you press SAVE yourself.

If the page plays but the FM-1 doesn’t, open the FM-1 panel and press Play a test note on the FM-1, then check the output port. If the FM-1 plays but its purple trace never appears, choose its audio input from the list; its name should include “FM-1”.

The control numbers the page uses to set the FM-1 come from the MIDI chapter of Baud Girl’s manual. The panels send Virtual Analog settings and four effects. FM presets accept some MIDI controls too (Brightness, Feedback, Algorithm, envelope and LFO), but the FM lesson’s demo is a simplified two-operator model rather than the FM-1’s six-operator engine, and the manual lists no MIDI controls for the 8-Bit engine. So on those two lessons the FM-1 connection is for playing and listening.
