Lesson 11

Playing it live

Designing a sound is half the job. The other half is playing it: sweeping a filter as a chord swells, pushing a lead into distortion for one phrase, opening a reverb at the end of a section. The FM-1+VA was built for exactly this, and almost all of it runs through four knobs.

Four knobs, teneleven banks

KNOB1 to KNOB4 always do something, on any screen. A knob bank decides what. There are teneleven:

  • Preset: four settings you choose for each preset, saved with it.
  • One bank per effect (Filter, Reverb, Delay, Distortion, Chorus, Phaser, Bitcrush): that effect’s three main settings on KNOB1 to KNOB3, and its on/off switch on KNOB4.
  • Global: Tempo, Arp Rate, Arp Gate and Glide Time.
  • Mixer: the preset’s level and the metronome’s, or on an 8-Bit preset the drums, bass and lead levels.
  • MIDI CC (SMK-37 only): KNOB1 to KNOB4 send CC 74, 71, 73 and 72 on the Out Channel, for playing other gear.

Two ways to switch banks:

  1. On the HOME screen, press HOME to open the Knob Banks menu, turn SELECT to a bank and press SEL.
  2. Press KNOB BANK to open the Knob Banks menu, turn SELECT to a bank and press SEL.
  3. From any screen, hold HOME and turn SELECT. Each click steps to the next bank and the FM-1 names it. This is the one to learn for performing.

The bank belongs to the FM-1, not the preset, so it stays put when you change presets. Press SAVE and the FM-1 starts on that bank next time.

On the FM-1 the bank belongs to the instrument, not the preset, and saving makes it the starting bank. Check on your unit whether the SMK-37 keeps it across presets and after PATCH the same way.

Four faders, a bank of their own

The SMK-37 adds four faders, and they play a bank too, chosen separately from the knobs’. They start on the Mixer bank, so out of the box the faders set levels while K5 to K8 shape the sound.

  • Press FADER BANK to open the Fader Banks list. It offers the same banks as the knobs; choose one the way you choose a knob bank.
  • A split worth trying: knobs on the Preset bank for tone, faders on the Reverb or Delay bank for space. Both are under your hands at once, with no switching mid-phrase.
  • On the MIDI CC bank the faders send CC 7, 10, 91 and 93, the usual volume, pan, reverb and chorus numbers on other gear.

Choosing what the Preset knobs play

On the Preset bank, each knob can play one of eight settings, depending on the engine:

  • Virtual Analog: Cutoff, Resonance, Filter Env, Filter Decay, Shape, Super, Detune, LFO to Cutoff. A new preset starts with the first four.
  • FM: Brightness, Feedback, Attack, Decay, Release, Vibrato, LFO Speed, Cutoff. A new preset starts with Brightness, Feedback, Attack and Release.
  • 8-Bit: Drum Decay, Bass Arpeggio, Lead Arpeggio, Lead Decay, Lead Arp Speed, Lead Vibrato, Lead Release, Bass Decay. A new preset starts with the first four.

To change one, stay on the Preset bank, hold HOME and turn the knob you want to reassign; each click steps to the next choice and the FM-1 names it. Or set all four at once in the Knobs group near the end of the EDIT list. Then SAVE.

One thing to know before a gig: on Virtual Analog and 8-Bit presets, a knob changes notes that are already sounding, so you hear the sweep under your fingers. On FM presets, a knob change applies from the next note you play.

Three setups worth saving

  • The filter player: Preset bank with Cutoff, Resonance, Filter Env and LFO to Cutoff. Good for basses and pads.
  • The wide lead: Cutoff, Filter Env, Super and Detune. Thin it for verses, open it up for the chorus.
  • The grit and space switcher: keep the Preset bank for tone, and hold HOME and turn SELECT to jump to the Distortion bank (Gain, Tone, Level, on/off) or the Reverb bank (type, Decay, Mix, on/off) for a moment, then back.

Getting your hands free

The FM-1 has 27 small keys and four knobs right next to each other, so you can’t easily hold a chord with one hand and play much else with it.Even with 37 keys, a hand holding a chord is a hand that isn’t on the knobs, faders or wheels. Three ways around that:

  • Arpeggiator with Latch. Press ARP, set Latch to On, play a chord and let go. It keeps playing, and both hands are free for the knobs. The next chord you play replaces it.
  • The sequencer. Record a pattern, press PLAY, and perform on the knobs over it. Patterns can be up to 64 steps and chained into songs.Patterns can be chained into songs.
  • Long envelopes. A pad with a long release keeps sounding after you lift your hand, buying you a moment to reach for a knob.

Pads and wheels

The SMK-37’s sixteen pads have three banks. Press KNOB BANK and FADER BANK together (the panel prints the pair as PAD BANK) to step to the next one. They start on Sequencer.

  • Sequencer: each pad moves the cursor to a step on the current pattern page.
  • Notes: each pad plays a note, as loud as you strike it. On an 8-Bit preset the pads play the drums and sound effects, so one hand can drum while the other plays the lead.
  • Presets: tap a pad to load the preset stored on it; hold a pad and turn K3 to store one there. Sixteen sounds a tap away is a quick way to change sound between sections. Turning K3 drops unsaved changes without asking, so press PATCH before you change preset either way.

The PITCH wheel springs back to the middle; set its range with Bend Up and Bend Down in GLOBE. The MOD wheel stays where you leave it. Two power-on habits from the manual: leave the pitch wheel alone while switching on, since its position then becomes the center, and move the mod wheel to the bottom once so its full range is available. What the mod wheel moves on each engine, and whether pressing harder on a pad after the strike does anything (it didn’t in version 101), are worth checking on your unit.

Practice: one knob, one pass

Hold a chord below (or latch the arpeggiator on the FM-1 and turn on Mirror to FM-1), then spend a full minute moving only Cutoff. Slow sweeps, sudden drops, tiny wobbles. Then do the same with Resonance, then with Filter envelope. Learning what one knob can do on its own is how you find the moves you’ll actually use.

The middle picture is a spectrogram: time runs from right to left and pitch from bottom to top, so a minute of one knob leaves a trail you can read back. A filter sweep is a ceiling rising and falling over the harmonics; resonance is a bright line riding along its edge.

Practise on the SMK-37’s own knobs

With the SMK-37 connected, the practice panel follows its knobs, so the minute above can be spent on the real controls while the page draws them:

  • Switch on GLOBE › MIDI › Send CC. Every setting you change on the SMK-37 then moves here too: the slider glows purple, and the line under the panel names the setting and the number it came in on.
  • Or put the knobs on the MIDI CC bank: K5 to K8 then send CC 74, 71, 73 and 72, which the page reads as Cutoff, Resonance, Attack and Release.
  • The pitch wheel bends the page’s synth as far as the SMK-37 bends its own (12 semitones, the SMK-37’s starting Bend Up and Bend Down), the mod wheel adds vibrato from the panel’s LFO, and the sustain pedal holds notes.
  • Press Focus on the panel to fill the screen with it, scopes and spectrogram included, while you play.

Adding a controller

The FM-1 listens to MIDI control messages, so a keyboard or controller with knobs can play it too. The FM-1+VA manual lists them, and a few are especially handy for performing:

The SMK-37 listens to MIDI control messages too, so another keyboard or controller with knobs can play it. The SMK-37+VA manual’s MIDI chapter lists the same numbers as the FM-1+VA’s for the Virtual Analog settings and the effects, which are the ones the page sends. A few worth knowing:

  • CC 85 to 88 turn KNOB1 to KNOB4.
  • CC 85 to 88 turn K5 to K8, as they turn the FM-1’s four knobs. Its buttons, and stepping K1 to K3, have numbers too (CC 89, 90 and 102 to 119); only PATCH has none.
  • On a Virtual Analog preset, CC 74 sets Cutoff and CC 71 Resonance, the numbers many controllers already send from their first knobs.
  • Notes, velocity and pitch bend work as usual, and the mod wheel and aftertouch push the LFO. Keep your controller on the FM-1’s MIDI channel, not its FX channel (2 by default), or some controls are read as effect settings.

There’s deliberately no MIDI control for SAVE, so a controller’s knobs can never overwrite a preset by accident. The full list is in the MIDI chapter of Baud Girl’s manual.

A two-minute routine

  1. Load the warm pad or acid bass from lesson 10.
  2. Set the Preset knobs to Cutoff, Resonance, Filter Env and LFO to Cutoff, and SAVE.
  3. Press ARP, turn on Latch, choose a slow Rate, and play a chord.
  4. Play with the four knobs for a minute. Then hold HOME, turn SELECT to Knobs: Reverb, and bring the Mix up for the ending.
  5. Press ARP again to stop.
