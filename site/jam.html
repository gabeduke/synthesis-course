Jam

Pick a key and a mode and press Play. The page makes a drone that drifts slowly through a few chords in that key, never quite the same twice, for you to play over on guitar, keys or anything else. Plug in an instrument and it plays a part along with it.

How to use it

  • On a phone. Plug the instrument into the phone with a USB-C cable (or an OTG adapter), open this page in Chrome and tap Connect USB MIDI. Android usually sends the phone’s sound to a USB audio device once it’s plugged in, so the drone may come out of the instrument’s headphone jack along with its own part.
  • Without a cable. Put the SMK-37 in BT Device mode and tap Connect over Bluetooth. The page sends the part over Bluetooth MIDI, a little ahead of time so it stays in step. It needs Chrome on Android, Windows, macOS or ChromeOS; iPhones can’t do it.
  • Which part. Hold turns the instrument into a second drone that changes chord with the page. Pulse and Bass give you something to lock onto. Arp and Wander play lines you can answer. Send a matching sound sets a pad (for Hold) or a pluck (for the rest) on the FM-1 or SMK-37’s current preset, using lesson 10’s recipes.
  • Choosing a mode. They’re listed bright to dark. Dorian and Mixolydian are the friendliest to improvise in on guitar; the drone leans on each mode’s color note (Dorian’s 6th, Lydian’s ♯4) so you can hear what makes it different.
  • MIDI clock lets gear that follows it (an arpeggiator, a delay, a Bento) run at the page’s tempo. Leave it off if the instrument doesn’t need it.

Lesson 12 is about building drones on the instrument itself. Once you have one you like, set the part here to Pulse or Wander and switch the page’s drone off, and the instrument carries the whole thing.
