A short history of synthesis

Every control on the FM-1 has a story behind it: a physicist, a studio trick, a commercial flop that became a genre. Here they are in order. Filter by family to follow one idea through time, and follow the links back to the lesson where you can hear it.

Sources

Dates were checked against manufacturer histories, patents, published papers and reference works. Where sources disagree, the timeline gives a range.

  • Fourier and Helmholtz: MacTutor History of Mathematics; Whipple Museum, Cambridge
  • Moog modules, ladder filter and ADSR: Bob Moog Foundation; US patent 3,475,623
  • FM synthesis: Stanford CCRMA; Chowning, “The Synthesis of Complex Audio Spectra by Means of Frequency Modulation”, Journal of the Audio Engineering Society 21(7), 1973
  • Reverb and echo: Sound On Sound; Universal Audio; Harvard Physics on Sabine
  • Chorus: Roland JC-120 history
  • TB-303: Roland
  • The Super Saw: Roland JP-8000; Adam Szabo, “How to Emulate the Super Saw”, KTH, 2010; Roland’s early trance listening guide
  • Band-limited oscillators: Brandt, “Hard Sync Without Aliasing”, ICMC 2001; Stilson and Smith, “Alias-Free Digital Synthesis of Classic Analog Waveforms”, ICMC 1996
  • The FM-1+VA firmware: Baud Girl
